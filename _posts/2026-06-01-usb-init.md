Welcome to my first post, where I explain [USB 2.0](https://www.usb.org/document-library/usb-20-specification) as fast as possible. USB is a PC to device protocol where you would connect to any device with least amount of reconfiguration (Plug and Play). USB also aims to be at the lowest cost for wider adoption.

The latest USB4 2.0 spec allows for crazy 80Gigabits/s and 240W on a type-C connector and has a lot of features like PCIE tunnelling, Displayport tunneling and host to host interface(Acting as Ethernet upto 10Gbps). All of the different versions of USB are still backwards compatible with USB 2.0 and for the embedded systems USB 2.0 High Speed seems good enough. I will mostly refer to USB 2.0 Spec when talking about USB from now. 

Since most of the people on the internet have covered the basics very well(look at [References](#references)), I will try to quickly go through it and then in the following post we can try what we learnt on [STM32F4](https://stm32-base.org/boards/STM32F407VET6-STM32-F4VE-V2.0.html).

## Architecture

The USB connects the following things:
 - Host: The main node that controls the bus. There can only be one host in the system. The main interface is called Host Controller and port which comes from host is called Host Root Hub. 
 - Devices: Also called as functions which extend the capability of the system. Eg: Joystick, Speakers etc. USB devices are required to carry information for self-identification and generic configuration.  They are also required at all times to display behavior consistent with defined USB device states.
 - Hubs: The USB bus follows a tiered star topology. Devices called hubs are at the center of each star. The maximum number of tiers allowed is seven.
 - Compound Device - Devices which act as multiple devices with an inbuilt hub.

## Electrical

USB 2.0 connects devices with 4 pairs of wires. 
 - +5V or VBUS providing power to devices. The amount of current is negotiated between Host and Device. Host can generally only provide power to neaby devices. The devices can be self-powered by having its own power supply too. A Hub can also seperately provide power.
 - GND
 - D+ and D- Differential allows for signalling at High Speed(@480Mbps), Full Speed(@12Mbps) and Low Speed(@1.5Mbps)

The port on device side is called upstream port while on host side is called downstream port.

Notice that if you are coming from something like SPI - there is no clock line. The clock is transmitted as part of SYNC field in transmission of every packet allowing recievers to synchronise their clocks. The encoding scheme is NRZI with bit stuffing to prevent clock drift.

The newer protocols include extra wires but still support this approach as it is all backwards compatible.

## Protocol
The Host initiates all the transfers. The Host on a scheduled basis would send a USB packet describing the type of transfer ,direction of the transaction, the USB Device Address, and endpoint number. This packet is known as __token packet__. The source of the transaction then sends a data packet or generates a NAK indicating it has no data to transfer. The destination then responds with ACK handshake or NAK indicating failure of transfer.

Based on the type of data transferred, there are two types of pipes: stream and message. Stream pipe has no USB defined data structure while message does. One message pipe, the Default Control Pipe, always exists once the device is powered thsi is how host is able to get device's configuration, status and control.

There are 4 type of transfers.


 - Control Transfers:  Used to configure a device at attach time and can be used for other device-specific purposes, including control of other pipes on the device. Data delivery is lossless.
 - Bulk Data Transfers:  Generated or consumed in relatively large and bursty quantities and have wide dynamic latitude in transmission constraints. Reliable exchange of data is ensured at the hardware level by using error detection in hardware and invoking a limited number of retries in hardware. 
 - Interrupt Data Transfers: A limited-latency transfer to or from a device is referred to as interrupt data.  Such data may be presented for transfer by a device at any time and is delivered by the USB at a rate no slower than is specified by the device. 
 - Isochronous Data Transfers:  Occupy a prenegotiated amount of USB bandwidth with a prenegotiated delivery latency.  (Also called streaming real time transfers).
